# Bluebook Citation Defaults

This reference condenses the Bluebook 21st edition Bluepages for practitioner documents: briefs, motions, and legal memoranda. Local court rules and the user's assignment rules control when they differ.

## 1. Choose the Right Set of Rules

- Use the Bluepages for court documents and practitioner memoranda.
- Use the Whitepages rules 1-21 for law review or academic work.
- Check jurisdiction-specific rules in Bluepages Table BT2 and the court's local rules before filing.
- Use italics or underscoring consistently in court documents. The Bluepages traditionally underscore.

For court documents, italicize or underscore:

- case names, including procedural phrases;
- titles of books, articles, and some legislative materials;
- introductory signals;
- explanatory phrases introducing subsequent history such as `aff'd` or `rev'd`;
- internal cross-references such as `id.` and `supra`; and
- phrases introducing related authority such as `quoted in`.

## 2. Citation Sentences and Clauses

- A citation sentence follows the proposition, begins with a capitalized signal where one is used, and ends with a period. It supports the whole preceding sentence.
- A citation clause follows only part of a sentence and is set off by commas. Signals are lowercase.
- Multiple authorities in one citation sentence are separated by semicolons.

Example:

> The Supreme Court established judicial review. Marbury v. Madison, 5 U.S. (1 Cranch) 137, 177-79 (1803).

## 3. Signals

Use the signal that accurately describes the relationship between proposition and authority.

| Signal | Use |
| --- | --- |
| No signal | The authority directly states the proposition, is the source of a quotation, or was identified in the proposition. |
| `E.g.,` | One of multiple authorities directly stating the proposition. |
| `Accord` | One of multiple authorities directly stating or supporting the proposition; another cited authority was identified in the proposition. |
| `See` | The authority supports, but does not directly state, the proposition. |
| `See also` | The authority provides additional supporting material. |
| `Cf.` | The authority is analogous enough to lend support. |
| `Compare ... with ...` | The cited authorities reveal meaningful similarities or differences. |
| `Contra` | The authority directly states a contrary proposition. |
| `But see` | The authority clearly supports a contrary proposition. |
| `But cf.` | The authority supports a proposition analogous to the contrary proposition. |
| `See generally` | The authority is helpful background. |

Order different signals and their authorities in the Bluebook signal order: no signal, `e.g.,`, `accord`, `see`, `see also`, `cf.`, `compare`, `contra`, `but see`, `but cf.`, `see generally`. Within a signal, put the more authoritative or helpful sources first.

`e.g.,` may combine with `see` or `but see`: `see, e.g.,`; `but see, e.g.,`.

## 4. Explanatory Parentheticals

Add a parenthetical only when it explains the citation's relevance.

- Begin with a present participle: `(holding that ...)`, `(explaining that ...)`, `(finding that ...)`.
- A quoted full sentence may stand alone with normal sentence capitalization and punctuation.
- Place the explanatory parenthetical after the date and before any subsequent history.

Example:

> Green v. Georgia, 442 U.S. 95, 97 (1979) (per curiam) (holding that exclusion of relevant evidence at a sentencing hearing constitutes denial of due process).

## 5. Pinpoint Cites and Subdivisions

- Give page numbers before the date parenthetical, without `p.` or `at`.
- Cite a range as `101-02`, `1020-30`; omit repeated digits except the final two.
- Cite nonconsecutive pages as `103, 106-08, 132`.
- Cite a footnote as `199 n.4`.
- Cite sections with the `§` symbol: `42 U.S.C. § 1983`; multiple sections as `§§`.
- Cite footnotes or subdivisions exactly as the source organizes them.

## 6. Cases

### Full Citation

A full case citation has the case name, reporter volume and abbreviation, first page, pinpoint, and parenthetical with court and year, followed by any explanatory or weight parenthetical and subsequent history:

> Engel v. Vitale, 370 U.S. 421, 430 (1962).

> Thompson v. Hanson, 174 P.3d 120, 125 (Wash. Ct. App. 2007), aff'd, 239 P.3d 537 (Wash. 2009) (en banc).

### Case Names

- Keep only the first party on each side.
- Use only surnames for individuals.
- Omit first names, initials, `et al.`, and alternative names unless needed to avoid confusion.
- Abbreviate procedural phrases: `In re`, `ex rel.`, `Ex parte`.
- Abbreviate common words using Table T6 and geography using Table T10. Never abbreviate `United States` when it is a named party.
- In a textual sentence, use the full case name and only widely understood abbreviations. In a citation, use the citation form.
- Italicize or underscore the entire case name, but not the comma after it.

### Reporters and Court

- U.S. Supreme Court: cite `U.S.` when available, otherwise `S. Ct.`; omit the court name from the date parenthetical.
- Federal appellate: cite `F.`, `F.2d`, or `F.3d` and identify the circuit: `(9th Cir. 2003)`.
- Federal district: cite `F. Supp.`, `F. Supp. 2d`, or `F. Supp. 3d` and identify the district: `(S.D.N.Y. 1999)`.
- State cases: cite the regional reporter if available and identify the state or court: `590 N.W.2d 61 (Mich. 1999)`.
- Parallel citations: use them when local rules require, and give a pinpoint for each reporter.
- For state court documents, follow the jurisdiction's required reporters.

### Weight and History

- Add weight parentheticals after the date: `(5-4 decision)`, `(Breyer, J., dissenting)`, `(per curiam)`, `(en banc)`.
- Use Table T8 explanatory phrases for subsequent history: `aff'd`, `aff'g`, `rev'd`, `cert. denied`, `cert. granted`, `rev'd on other grounds`, `sub nom.`.
- Place an explanatory parenthetical before subsequent history.

### Short Forms

After a full citation in the same general discussion:

> Palsgraf v. Long Island R.R. Co., 162 N.E. 99 (N.Y. 1928)

Later forms include:

> Palsgraf, 162 N.E. at 100.

> 162 N.E. at 100.

> Id. at 100.

Use `id.` only when the immediately preceding citation contains a single authority. `Id.` alone means the same pinpoint; `id. at 8` means a different pinpoint. Capitalize `Id.` only at the start of a citation sentence.

## 7. Constitutions

> U.S. Const. art. I, § 8, cl. 10.

> U.S. Const. amend. XIV, § 1.

> Wash. Const. art. I, § 32.

Do not use short forms for constitutions other than `id.`.

## 8. Statutes, Rules, and Restatements

### Federal Codes

> 28 U.S.C. § 1331.

> 15 U.S.C.A. § 205 (West).

Cite the official U.S. Code when available. For an unofficial annotated code, include the publisher parenthetical. The year of the federal code edition may be omitted for `U.S.C.` and `U.S.C.A.`.

Include the original act section when useful:

> Administrative Procedure Act § 6, 5 U.S.C. § 555.

### Session Laws

> Department of Transportation Act, Pub. L. No. 89-670, § 9, 80 Stat. 931, 944-47 (1966).

Omit the year parenthetical when the act's name contains the year.

### State Codes

> Wash. Rev. Code § 28B.20.020 (2014).

> Cal. Penal Code § 181 (West 2011).

Use the preferred official code from Table T1.3 and the code edition year.

### Rules and Similar Materials

> Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(6).

> Fed. R. Evid. 401.

> Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 90 (Am. Law Inst. 1981).

> U.C.C. § 2-202 (Unif. Law Comm'n 1977).

## 9. Administrative and Legislative Materials

### Regulations

> 46 C.F.R. § 166.01 (2009).

For a pending or proposed regulation:

> Federal Acquisition Regulations for National Aeronautics and Space Administration, 55 Fed. Reg. 52,782 (Dec. 21, 1990) (to be codified at 48 C.F.R. pt. 1).

### Congressional Materials

> S. 1983, 93d Cong. § 10 (1973).

> Toxic Substances Control Act: Hearings on S. 776 Before the Subcomm. on the Env't of the S. Comm. on Commerce, 94th Cong. 343 (1975).

> H.R. Rep. No. 99-253, pt. 1, at 54 (1985).

## 10. Secondary Sources

### Books

> Matthew Butterick, Typography for Lawyers 54 (2010).

> David Hunter et al., International Environmental Law and Policy 555 (3d ed. 2006).

> 21 Charles Alan Wright & Arthur R. Miller, Federal Practice and Procedure § 1006 (3d ed. 1998).

Use `supra` for later references unless the work is the immediately preceding authority:

> Hunter et al., supra, at 560.

### Law Review Articles

For consecutively paginated journals:

> Fred R. Shapiro & Michelle Pearse, The Most-Cited Law Review Articles of All Time, 110 Mich. L. Rev. 1483, 1489 (2012).

For nonconsecutively paginated magazines:

> Christopher Hitchens, The New Commandments, Vanity Fair, Apr. 2011, at 1.

For student-written work, include the designation:

> Natalie Cotton, Comment, The Competence of Students as Editors of Law Reviews: A Response to Judge Posner, 154 U. Pa. L. Rev. 951, 982 n.104 (2006).

### Newspapers

> Abigail Sullivan Moore, This Is Your Brain on Drugs, N.Y. Times, Oct. 29, 2014, at A1.

## 11. Court and Litigation Documents

For documents filed in the same case, abbreviate the document title using Bluepages Table BT1:

> Pet'r's Br. 6.

> Trial Tr. vol. 2, 31.

> Clark Dep. 15:21-16:4.

> R. at 9.

> Def.'s Mot. Summ. J. 2, ECF No. 15.

Use a date when needed to distinguish documents or when it is relevant. Add the ECF number for electronically filed documents. Cite to the original document page number when it differs from the PACER header.

## 12. Internet Sources

> David Lat, Movie Night with Justice Breyer, Above the Law (Oct. 27, 2014, 3:31 PM), http://abovethelaw.com/2014/10/movie-night-with-justice-breyer/.

Include the author, specific page title, main site, date or timestamp when available, and URL. Use `(last visited ...)` when the page has no date. Prefer a stable PDF with pagination over an unpaginated HTML copy. Use `id.` or `supra` for later references.

## 13. Quotations and Alterations

- Quote exactly.
- Put commas and periods inside closing quotation marks; place other punctuation outside unless it is part of the quoted text.
- Mark omissions with an ellipsis and changes or insertions with brackets.
- For a quotation of fifty or more words, use a single-spaced block quotation indented on both sides without quotation marks; place the citation after the block at the left margin.
- Attribute a quotation inside another quotation to its original source when necessary.
- For a source quoted from another source, use the appropriate related-authority phrase such as `(quoting ...)` or `quoted in`.

## 14. Abbreviations and Tables

Do not invent legal abbreviations. Use:

- Table T1 for jurisdiction-specific reporters, codes, and administrative materials;
- Table T6 for common words in case names and institutional authors;
- Table T7 for court names;
- Table T8 for explanatory phrases;
- Table T10 for state, city, and geographical abbreviations;
- Table T13 for institutional names in periodical titles; and
- Table T16 for subdivisions.

Spell out numbers zero through ninety-nine in ordinary text. Use numerals for larger numbers, unless a larger number begins a sentence.

## 15. Citation Audit

When checking citations:

- Confirm the source exists and is good law.
- Confirm the reporter, volume, first page, pinpoint, court, year, and subsequent history.
- Confirm case names, abbreviations, signals, and parentheticals.
- Confirm the source organization's own subdivisions are used correctly.
- Reconcile every cited source with the Table of Authorities in a brief.
- Verify local court rules, especially when they require parallel citations or a different style.
